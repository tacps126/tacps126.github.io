Guide

How Stock Market Data & Technical Analysis works

Inputs, options and tips, from the tool's own documentation.

Enter tickers and get a full read of each one in a single row: price and returns, 25+ indicators, a technical rating, trend, support and resistance levels, pivot points, risk, expected price ranges, how each signal has worked on this ticker before, a plain-language assessment, plus fundamentals, news headlines and news and StockTwits sentiment. US, European and Japanese stocks and ETFs, indices, crypto and FX. No API key, no account.

How to use

  1. Add tickers, as on Yahoo Finance (AAPL, SPY, ^GSPC, 7203.T, SAP.DE, HSBA.L, MC.PA, BTC-USD) or TradingView (NASDAQ:AAPL, TSE:7203).
  2. Pick what to include and how much history (2 years by default; longer gives the backtests more cases).
  3. Press Start. Export to JSON, CSV or Excel, or send to Google Sheets, Slack or a webhook. Schedule it daily for a watchlist report.

Tool mode: your own indicators and rules

Everything above stays as it is. On top of it you can:

  • Pick indicators with your own settings: rsi:14, ema:50, macd:12,26,9, bb:20,2, supertrend:10,3, atrp, zscore:50, relvol:20 and 25 more. Each comes back as a column such as rsi_14 or bb_lower_20_2 under values, and on every price history row for charts.
  • Test your own rules on each ticker's history, one per line:
  • rsi_14 crosses above 30
  • bearish: close < sma_200 and rsi_14 > 60
  • Breakout = close > highest_20[1] and relvol_20 > 1.5

For each rule you get how many times it fired, how often the price then rose (or fell, for bearish rules) after 5 and 20 bars (or your own horizons), the average move, the base rate and the edge over it, when it last fired, and whether it is true now. Rules can use any indicator column, open, high, low, close, volume, numbers, + - * /, < > <= >= ==, and, or, not, crosses above, crosses below, is rising, is falling, abs(), min(), max(), and [n] for n bars ago. See Writing rules, or pick ready-made rules such as golden-cross or oversold-bounce.

  • Screen: return only tickers where one of your rules is true now (you pay only for those).
  • Get answers instead of data: output: answer returns per ticker a verdict (bullish to bearish) with confidence, a one-sentence answer, the reasons and what disagrees, support, resistance, volatility stops and the likely 5-day range.
  • Shape the output: flat gives one level of columns (rating.label, values.rsi_14, ...) for CSV, Sheets, n8n and AI agents, and fields returns only what you list.
json
{
  "tickers": ["AAPL", "NVDA", "SAP.DE"],
  "include": ["analysis"],
  "range": "5y",
  "indicators": ["rsi:14", "macd", "bb:20,2"],
  "rulePresets": ["golden-cross", "breakout-on-volume"],
  "rules": ["Oversold bounce = rsi_14 crosses above 30", "bearish: close < sma_200 and rsi_14 > 60"],
  "output": "flat",
  "fields": ["close", "rating.label", "values", "rules"]
}

It is built from the data the run already collected, opens in any browser, prints, and follows light or dark mode.

Writing rules

A rule is one line of plain text. Its shape:

code
[Name =] [bearish:] condition
  • Name (optional) is only a label for the output. Oversold bounce = rsi_14 crosses above 30 is the rule rsi_14 crosses above 30, shown as "Oversold bounce". Leave it out and the condition itself is the name.
  • bearish: (optional) means "I expect a fall after this". Rules are bullish by default: the backtest counts a win when the price rose afterwards; with bearish: it counts a win when the price fell. buy:/long: and sell:/short: work too.
  • condition is true or false on each bar. It fires on the bar where it turns true.

What a condition can use:

WriteMeaning
open, high, low, close, volumethe bar's prices and volume
any indicator column, e.g. rsi_14, sma_200see the table below; computed for you, no need to list it under Your indicators
numbers, + - * /, bracketsarithmetic, e.g. close > 1.05 * sma_50
< > <= >= == !=compare two values
and, or, notcombine conditions
a crosses above b, a crosses below btrue only on the bar where a moves from below b to above it (or the reverse)
x is rising, x is fallingx is higher (lower) than on the bar before
x[n]x as it was n bars ago: close > highest_20[1] means above the highest high of the 20 bars before today
abs(x), min(a, b), max(a, b)absolute value, smaller, larger

Indicator columns are the indicator's name followed by its settings, joined with _. Leave settings out to get the defaults shown here (so rsi is rsi_14, macd_hist is macd_hist_12_26_9).

Change the settings in the name to get another version: rsi_2, sma_50, bb_lower_20_3, highest_55.

Examples, read aloud:

  • rsi_14 crosses above 30: RSI climbs back above 30 (leaves oversold).
  • bearish: close < sma_200 and rsi_14 > 60: below the 200-day average but RSI is high; expect a fall.
  • Breakout = close > highest_20[1] and relvol_20 > 1.5: a new 20-bar high on 1.5 times usual volume.
  • close > sma_200 and rsi_2 < 10: a sharp short dip inside a long uptrend.
  • abs(roc_1) > 2 * stdev_20: a day that moved more than twice the usual daily move.

Good to know

  • The rating, levels, ranges and backtests describe the chart and its history. They are not investment advice and past signals do not promise future moves.
  • Backtests count every time a rule fired in the chosen history and measure the move 5 and 20 bars later, with no costs; compare the hit rate with the base rate shown next to it.
  • Fundamentals, news and sentiment cover listed stocks and ETFs; for crypto, FX and indices you get the price analysis. StockTwits sentiment covers US tickers.
  • Prices come from public quote pages and can be delayed by up to 15-20 minutes during market hours.
Questions

Questions about Stock Market Data & Technical Analysis

Which markets?

Any symbol Yahoo Finance knows: US, Europe (Germany, UK, France, Netherlands, Italy, Spain, Switzerland, Nordics), Japan, plus Canada, Australia, Hong Kong, Korea, India, Taiwan, Singapore.

How do the ratings compare to TradingView's?

The rating follows the same published approach (moving averages vs. price, oscillator rules); TradingView's own number is included under fundamentals for comparison.

Can I get intraday data?

Yes, set Bar size to Hourly (up to 2 years of history).

How much does Stock Market Data & Technical Analysis cost?

$3 per 1,000, charged only for ticker analysiss returned; a run that finds nothing costs nothing. It runs on Apify, whose free plan includes monthly credit, so you can try it without paying.

Do I need to write code?

No. Fill in the form on Apify and press Start, schedule it, or send the results to Google Sheets, Slack or a webhook. For code, the input builder on this page writes the API call in curl, Python or JavaScript.

What does Stock Market Data & Technical Analysis return?

Every row has the same fields, including Price, Assessment, Technical rating, Trend, Levels, Indicators, Risk and ranges, Signals (backtested). Download as JSON, CSV, Excel or XML.

Can an AI agent use it?

Yes. The webdata-tools-mcp server exposes it to Claude, Cursor and other MCP clients as get_stock_market_data.
