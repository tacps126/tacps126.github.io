Short Interest & Short Volume — FINRA, All US Stocks

FINRA short interest for every US stock (NYSE, Nasdaq, Arca, Cboe, OTC): short interest, change, days to cover, average volume, plus daily short-sale volume and short ratio. By ticker with one year of history, or the whole market with filters.

Try it

Build the input, get the call

Change any field; the code on the right follows. Copy it, or run the same input on Apify.

Short interest is the number of shares held short on a settlement date (the 15th and the month end). Daily short volume is the shares sold short each trading day.

Stock tickers, e.g. GME or BRK.A. Leave empty for the whole market (about 22,000 stocks; use the filters below).

Short interest settlement date as YYYY-MM-DD, e.g. 2026-10-15. Empty = the newest published.

For tickers: how many settlement dates back (24 = one year of history).

Add each stock's share of volume sold short over the trading days before the settlement date (`shortVolumeRatio`).

Daily short volume: how many trading days back. With "Add short volume ratio": how many days are averaged.

Filters

Short interest only. Empty = every market.

Short interest divided by average daily volume, e.g. 5.

e.g. 1000000.

e.g. 20 for short interest up 20% or more; -100 keeps everything.

Leaves out thinly traded stocks, e.g. 100000.

Share of volume sold short, 0 to 1, e.g. 0.5.

Daily short volume only: leave out days with less volume.

Newest date first, then this (highest first).

Most rows returned.

Monitoring

Schedule the Actor and each run returns only rows (ticker + date) that earlier runs did not deliver: each new settlement date or trading day once.

AI insights (optional)

Add an `insight` to each row: what the short position says, the squeeze risk and what to watch; with a profile, how relevant it is to you.

What you trade or watch, e.g. "Small-cap biotech swing trader, looks for short squeezes with rising short interest". Adds a fit score.

Insights are written in this language, whatever the source language.

Insights are added to the first N rows (in the sort order).
